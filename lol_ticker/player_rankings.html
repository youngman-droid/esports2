How to read these ratings Methodology & coverage

A lineup model, with recency and shrinkage

A regularized logistic lineup model fits game wins directly. Teammates and opponents are modeled together, with champion-role and blue-side adjustments by league and patch. Shared competition coefficients are learned from international lineup comparisons. Recent matches carry more weight. Individual effects shrink toward zero; no gold statistics or lane priors enter this fit.

Impact is estimated wins added per 100 otherwise evenly matched games versus a zero-effect player: +5 means 55 expected wins instead of 50. This is a standardized rate, not cumulative career wins or a causal replacement estimate. Lineup contributions add in log odds; displayed wins-added rates do not add together. Role score is a separate comparison within each role: 50 is the role average, and 10 points is one standard deviation. It is not a probability or a cross-role impact measure.

Career by age shows the latest eligible fitted snapshot per player per calendar year, with at least 20 decayed games. Age is calculated at that snapshot. A player can appear in many age bands. The era score is (role score − 50) / 10: standard deviations from the same-role, recency-weighted reference at that historical fit. This compares relative standing, not absolute skill. Recent seasons use monthly fits; older seasons use annual fits. Age bands use small deterministic horizontal jitter for visibility, never to change age.

Region groups domestic circuits geographically at the selected snapshot, not by player nationality. Asia-Pacific includes Japan, Vietnam, Pacific and Oceania. International-only observations use the most recent known domestic snapshot available by that date; unresolved affiliations remain explicit. The source circuit stays in CSV exports and region-cell tooltips.

Intervals describe conditional model uncertainty. They do not capture every source of error or prove that one player is better than another. Shared rosters, limited cross-league play, and unobserved context can confound individual effects. Players who consistently share a lineup cannot be cleanly separated by match results alone.

Decayed games sum recency weights and show how much match evidence remains. “Active” is relative to the model's data cutoff. This view ranks players present in the source data; it cannot cover unrecorded players or matches. Recency and shrinkage are DARKO-inspired; this is an exploratory LoL model.
